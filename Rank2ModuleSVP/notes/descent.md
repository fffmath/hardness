## Proofs and interfaces

`Descent2` works in the actual round-1 integral coefficient quotient. Evaluation
of integer polynomials is surjective, and its kernel is generated by `Φ_q`.
Iterating `X * derivative` identifies power sums with evaluations at one;
below degree `q`, multiplicity decreases by exactly one under this operator.
This proves the power-sum divisibility criterion without complex embeddings or
an assumed Hasse-derivative change of basis.

Modulo `q`, the prime relation is `(X−1)^(q−1)`. Lifting this identity gives
`Φ_q = (X−1)^(q−1) + qG`, with `G(1)=1`. An induction on powers of `π` proves
`π^(q−1) | q`; the same relation gives the converse principal-ideal inclusion.
These facts establish both directions of descent and the polynomial preimage
criterion. A Vandermonde minor constructs every compatible syndrome; subtracting
a constant vector adjusts its integral coefficient sum.

`BennettPeikertMinimum` remains the round-1 definition of a proposition, but
`bennettPeikertMinimum` in `BennettPeikert.lean` proves that proposition outright. A hypothetical vector below `2k` has sum zero and equal
positive/negative masses `m < k`. Dependent finite types index repeated positive
and negative occurrences. Newton identities, evaluated in `ZMod q`, equate the
elementary symmetric polynomials through order `m`, because `m < q`. Vieta's
formula equates the monic root polynomials. Evaluating at a positive occurrence
contradicts disjointness of the positive and negative supports.
The reusable `ramifiedIdeal_norm_lower` in `Descent2` retains its explicit `hBP`
argument; `ramifiedIdeal_minimum` in `BennettPeikert` supplies the proof and is
unconditional.

`robust_center_explicit` assumes `100 ≤ k`, `k ≤ q−1`, and `4 ≤ q`, and permits
any natural `h`. Sparsification gives at most `k/25` coefficients for a proposed
nearby point and at most six for the short multiplier. Two applications of
`sparse_tools_ii` give the contradiction. A short nonzero multiplier cannot have
zero residue polynomial: divisibility of all coefficients by `q` would make its
positive integral norm at least `q² > 3q`.

The v2 separation interface uses `sepK0 = 100`, `sepC0 = 1`, and
`sepWeight k = 21*k/20`. `idealDistanceSq` is the attained natural-number
minimum of canonical squared norms over an ideal; `idealDistanceSq_attained`
realizes that minimum. `robust_center_distance` and `separation_distance_v2`
prove `2*k*q < 10*idealDistanceSq`, the v2 lower bound `L/10`.
No Wan estimate is needed for separation.
