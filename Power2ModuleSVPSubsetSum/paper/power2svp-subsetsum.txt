                                            Abstract
          We prove deterministic NP-completeness of exact shortest-vector decision in the coef-
     ficient maximum norm for full-rank free rank-two modules over power-of-two cyclotomic
     rings. Our reduction from binary Subset Sum uses an explicit multiplication operator to
     force the ring multiplier of every short vector to be a signed monomial. The construction
     has polynomial binary size and also yields NP-hardness of exact shortest-vector search.


1    Introduction
Lattice-based cryptography is a central approach to post-quantum security, as illustrated by
the standardization of ML-KEM and ML-DSA [10, 11]. Its security relies on the presumed
hardness of lattice problems and related average-case problems, with worst-case-to-average-
case reductions providing connections between them. Regev’s quantum reduction from
worst-case lattice approximation problems to learning with errors is a fundamental exam-
ple [12]. The complexity of basic lattice problems depends on the norm and the required
approximation factor. Van Emde Boas established deterministic NP-hardness for exact
closest-vector problems and exact maximum-norm SVP [13]. For Euclidean SVP, Ajtai
proved NP-hardness under randomized reductions [1], and Micciancio proved       √ randomized
hardness of approximation within every fixed factor strictly between one and 2 [9]. Recent
preprints of Wan report deterministic hardness for exact Euclidean SVP and for approximation
within every fixed constant factor greater than one [14, 15].
    Algebraic structure permits compact representations and efficient arithmetic in lattice-
based cryptography. It also imposes a substantive restriction on lattice hardness: a module
contains all ring multiples of each vector, so a reduction must control more vectors than the
integer combinations used in an ordinary lattice encoding. The ring and module worst-case-
to-average-case reductions [7, 5] motivate studying these structured families. General lattice
NP-hardness does not automatically persist under their algebraic restrictions.
    Several recent results address this question for structured lattices. Liu, Feng, and Pan
prove deterministic NP-completeness for exact SVP in the canonical Euclidean norm on free
rank-two modules over Z[ζq ], where q ≡ 3 (mod 4) is prime [6]. Martin gives a dimension-
preserving reduction from general Euclidean lattice problems to ideal-lattice problems in
varying totally real monogenic orders [8]. Wan proves deterministic NP-completeness for
coefficient Euclidean SVP on cyclic lattices associated with Z[X]/(X N − 1), where N = q − 1
for varying odd primes [16]. These results concern different algebraic families or norms from
the one studied here.

    Our module construction retains the Boolean expression as 2a − ub, where a, b ∈ Rn and
u marks the positions of the weights. The additional difficulty is that the homogenizing
multiplier b is now a polynomial. Even a bound kbk∞ ≤ 1 allows many coefficients to be

to belong to

the norm and reduces the problem to the Boolean encoding with b = 1.

opposite pair. A second multiplication operator is skew-adjoint, meaning that its adjoint
is its negative for the coefficient inner product. The short-vector inequalities make certain
products nonpositive, while skew-adjointness makes their sum zero. Each product must
consequently vanish. Powers of five separate the individual shift tests, and tests at both
positions of an occupied pair force every other coefficient to vanish. After this normalization,
reversed weights encode the subset sum in a constant coefficient, and an opposite coefficient
imposes the reverse inequality. Together they force equality with the target.
     Section 2 fixes the algebraic and computational definitions. Section 3 proves multiplier
rigidity. Section 4 gives the reduction, proves its correctness and polynomial size, and derives
witness recovery and search hardness. Section 5 concludes with the scope of the construction.

5    Conclusion
We proved deterministic NP-completeness of exact SVP in the coefficient maximum norm for
full-rank free rank-two modules over power-of-two cyclotomic rings. The reduction combines
the Boolean encoding of Subset Sum with an explicit multiplier filter. This filter controls
every ring multiplier of a short vector, permitting normalization by a signed monomial and
recovery of a satisfying assignment. The same construction also establishes NP-hardness of
exact shortest-vector search.
    The proof uses individual coefficient bounds to turn skew-adjoint orthogonality into
vanishing conditions. Extending this construction to finite-p norms or to rank-one ideals
would require a different way to obtain this rigidity or to enforce the Boolean constraints. A
fixed relative approximation gap would likewise require stronger norm separation than the
additive gap established here.
