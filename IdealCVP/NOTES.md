# Reduction formalization

The LaTeX sources under `paper/src/chapter/` (`pre.tex`,
`principal-ideal.tex`, `sivp-module.tex`, and `fixed.tex`) are the specification. Indices are
zero-based. Integer coefficient vectors carry the ordinary squared Euclidean
norm. Rounds 1–5 establish the mathematical reductions and certificate bounds.
Round 6 adds native complexitylib NP membership and SAT-to-masked hardness.
Round 7 closes the compiler runtime obligation with a certified cubic list
compiler and proves unconditional NP-completeness.
Round 8 proves list X3C NP-complete, discharges the paper's X3C route, and
certifies the principal cyclic CVP hardness lift.
Round 9 proves NP-completeness for a recognized triangular module-SIVP subclass
and the fixed-family CVPP Karp–Lipton corollary, including universe-indexed
polynomial preprocessing advice.
Round 10 proves NP-completeness of principal cyclic CVP on a polynomial-time
recognized integral separated class containing the reduction image.

## Round 2 changes and size

The canonical `X3CInstance.reductionData`, and hence `x3cToCVP`, now use
`SidonPositions.erdosTuran`. The ambient dimension is O(Mn²), as in the
paper, with the explicit bound above. The formulas for h, t, the parity
penalty, threshold, gap, and the existing correctness theorems are unchanged.
The Sidon formula and prime search are executable; no classical choice is
used to select the prime. The singleton case still has c=0.

The ring connection is now proved, rather than an informal interpretation
of the coefficient model. No ring structure is postulated on the vectors:
the additive bijection and multiplication compatibility connect them directly
to Mathlib's quotient ring. Round 3 adds irreducibility and general full rank;
Round 4 proves canonical-embedding norm scaling in `Canonical.lean`. The original
compiler-specific full-rank proof via norm estimates is retained as an
independent result. The cyclic lift now also uses the general domain proof.
The cyclic dimension is exactly twice the cyclotomic dimension, and its
squared distances are multiplied by eight.

## Round 3 changes and remaining scope

General full rank, the module-SIVP completion and reduction, and the fixed
masked-family combinatorial reduction are now proved. All new files are
imported by `IdealCVP.lean`. The dimension-one case is included in the
power-of-two and module theorems. Cyclic full rank needs no supplied
injectivity proof for nonzero power-of-two generators.

The module development works in the genuine integral quotient pairs,
embedded injectively in the genuine rational quotient field pairs. Thus its
independence predicate is over K_d, rather than over ℤ or ℚ. Norms refer to
the coefficient embedding throughout. Exact minima assert both attainment
and the lower bound for every independent pair.

Round 4 closes the canonical embedding, rational-data, and indexed repeated-triple
conversion gaps listed after Round 3. Remaining scope is described below. There
are no remaining `sorry` placeholders.

`W_le`, `weight_le`, `cross_card`, and `t_norm` give additional size and norm
calculations. No complexity class, NP-completeness, or machine runtime
assertion is encoded as a theorem; “polynomial” here refers to the explicit
mathematical dimension bounds.

## Round 4 changes and remaining scope

All four Round 4 task groups are represented by the
five new files above, imported by `IdealCVP.lean`. Existing proven source files
and the Lake configuration are unchanged. Rational scaling multiplies the
lattice, target, and squared threshold together; the floor is applied only
once the target and lattice are integral. Arbitrary rational module generators
are reduced to an actual integral-ring span, preserving field independence.
Canonical norms are evaluations at the odd roots, with a proved Fourier
identity, rather than a definition as d times the coefficient norm.

The certificate result is deliberately scoped to verification and produced
instances. No polynomial witness bound for arbitrary integer basis matrices,
complexity-class NP membership, Turing machine runtime, NP-completeness,
CVPP, P/poly, or Karp–Lipton theorem is asserted. The data-only compiler and
its explicit polynomial magnitude bounds do not themselves formalize runtime.
No Round 4 mathematical task has been left with a placeholder or an unfinished
proof; heavy complexity-class infrastructure remains outside scope as requested.
